01PROBLEM

Why invoice intake needs a control layer.

In a typical small Accounts Payable team, invoices arrive as PDFs in different layouts. The problem is less the volume than the exceptions: a missing purchase order, a wrong quantity or a duplicate can reach payment if each check depends on someone reading every document.

  • AS-ISA clerk opens each PDF, re-types the fields, looks up the purchase order and chases Procurement by email. Exceptions live in personal inboxes.
  • TO-BEThe workflow reads and checks every invoice. Complete invoices go to an approval report; the clerk works only on an exception queue that states the reason, the priority and a suggested next action.

This is a typical process described for the prototype, not a documented process from a specific company.

02PROCESS

From inbox noise to an actionable queue.

The workflow is designed around the operational exception, not around the model. Standard invoices can move forward; anything uncertain is routed to a person instead of being guessed.

  1. IngestRead a PDF from a local inbox folder.
  2. ExtractCapture invoice number, supplier, purchase order, total and VAT.
  3. ValidateCheck the five required fields, then apply duplicate, PO and quantity rules.
  4. RouteWrite the invoice to the approval report or to the human review queue.
03TEST RUN

What happened on six test invoices.

I ran the n8n workflow locally on six fictional invoice PDFs. Two were complete and went to the approval report. Four went to review:

  • INV-2002, INV-2004VAT missing from the document.
  • INV-2005Supplier missing from the document.
  • INV-2001The supplier was on the invoice, but in a different layout (the label on its own line above the name). The parser did not recognise it, so the invoice went to review rather than receiving a guessed value.

The INV-2001 case is the most useful result: layout variety, not missing data, is the main risk for this kind of automation. Before scaling, the parser needs tolerant rules for common layouts, and the review queue needs to stay in place.

05CONTROLS

Business rules before AI.

Every check is deterministic and explainable. No invoice is posted or paid automatically; human review remains mandatory before any financial action.

  • Required fieldsInvoice number, supplier, purchase order, total and VAT must all be found (n8n workflow).
  • Duplicate checkThe same supplier and invoice number cannot be processed twice (Python rules).
  • PO and quantity checkA missing purchase order, or a quantity above the one ordered, routes to review (Python rules). A full three-way match with goods receipts is a next step.
  • Confidence gateLow extraction confidence or missing VAT always routes to review (Python rules).
  • Result logEach result is stored in SQLite with issue, priority, suggested action and timestamp.
06MEASURES

How I would measure a pilot.

The test run is too small to claim savings. In a real pilot I would track these measures from the first week, against the current manual process:

  • Straight-through rateShare of invoices that need no human touch (2 of 6 in the test run).
  • Exception mixReasons for review, to see whether problems come from suppliers, purchase orders or the parser.
  • Cycle timeTime from invoice receipt to approval, for standard invoices and for exceptions.
  • Duplicates caughtDuplicate invoices stopped before payment.
07BUILD

Free and local by design.

Built so far: a local n8n workflow for PDF intake, field extraction and routing; Python business rules with SQLite persistence; and this browser interface for the exception queue. No real invoices, credentials or paid APIs are used.
